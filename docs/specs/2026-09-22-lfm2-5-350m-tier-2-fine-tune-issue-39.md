# LFM2.5-350M Tier 2 fine-tune (issue 39)

> A fine-tuned LFM2.5-350M ends Tier 2 turns with propose or escalate where those are right, and still explains read-only questions in words: it beats the stock 350M on right proposals and escalations, makes no wrong mutating proposals, and keeps a warm median under half a second on the DGX Spark

## Audience

- The operator at an nvsh prompt on a Jetson or DGX Spark with \[tiers\] enabled, who gets a local proposal or a clean hand-off to the full agent instead of a paragraph of words; and the nvsh maintainer who has to reproduce the tuning run and its measurement.
  - instruction: check docs/lfm-finetune.md names both readers: the operator who enables the tuned model and the maintainer who re-runs the recipe

## Before → After

- Before: Stock LFM2.5-350M through the real launcher and vLLM made 0 of 53 right proposals and 1 of 27 escalations, with 0 wrong mutating proposals and a 218 ms warm median, on the 80-entry fold (docs/benchmarks/2026-09-19-dgx-spark-tier2.md; issue 39). Those numbers came from a scratch script around nvsh.tiers.bench.bench, not a committed command (lapse l9, issue 40).
- After: docs/lfm-finetune.md becomes a guide to fine-tuning LFM2.5-350M successfully, where successful means the tuned model measurably improves on the target, not merely that training runs: on the committed test fold it beats stock re-measured on the same fold on right proposals and on escalations, with 0 wrong mutating proposals, explain kept where words are right, and a warm median under 500 ms (c26, c27). The guide covers every step needed to reach and prove that: environment setup on the DGX Spark; splitting the training data with the committed seeded split script (train/test, stratified by expectation, held-out refused); chat-template check; building the training set from the train side only; training with the hyperparameters that worked; merge; export check against training examples; publishing to a private repo; measuring stock and tuned with the committed measurement script; and reading the result against the success criteria -- plus the pitfalls hit and how each was fixed. The resulting checkpoint is served by pointing \[tiers.lfm\] model at it, with no nvsh runtime change.
  - instruction: follow docs/lfm-finetune.md top to bottom in a fresh venv: split, build, train, merge, export check, measure; the measured stock-vs-tuned table in the guide must show c26 and c27 met

## Why it matters

- Tier 2 exists to answer locally in about a fifth of a second, but a Tier 2 that never proposes or escalates just adds latency before the operator re-asks the full agent; the operator chose (deviation d4) to keep the 350M for its footprint and teach it the control tools rather than move to the 1.2B.
  - instruction: cite docs/tier2.md and deviation d4 in the tiered-local-response frame

## Success signals

- Use-case bar: on the committed test fold, through the real launcher and vLLM on the DGX Spark, the tuned 350M reaches >= 70% right proposals and >= 60% correct escalations, above stock re-measured on the same fold, with 0 wrong mutating proposals and a warm median < 500 ms (indicative; formal acceptance is the held-out run, c21). Meeting this bar or the method-validation bar counts as success (decision on q6).
  - instruction: run the committed Tier 2 measurement for stock and tuned on the same test fold; compare the right-proposal, escalation, wrong-mutating and warm-median columns
- Explain examples keep words where words are right: on the test fold's explain-labelled read-only questions, the tuned model answers with explain (or plain text) at least as often as stock, and never turns one into a mutating proposal (0).
  - instruction: count, over explain-labelled test entries, outcomes that are Explanation vs proposals; any mutating proposal there fails
- Method validation on Jetson skill routing: each of NVIDIA's JetPack 7.2 agent skills (14 in jetson-device-skills, 24 in jetson-bsp-skills) becomes one tool whose description is its SKILL.md description, and a request is answered by calling the right skill. LFM2.5-350M tuned with this recipe beats stock on those 104 evals by a margin the guide states in advance, through the same chat-template check, export check and vLLM + lfm2 path as the nvsh run.
  - instruction: the guide names both repos and their commit shas, the 38 skills used as tools, the 104 eval ids as the test set, the pre-stated margin, and shows the stock-vs-tuned skill-routing table

- Trying a tuned model needs no nvsh runtime change: \[tiers.lfm\] model is passed through to the launcher and ToolChat (nvsh/agent/lfm.py:134-181), the loop is model-agnostic (nvsh/tiers/lfm.py:31-33), vLLM already launches with --tool-call-parser lfm2 by default (nvsh/tiers/`runtime_docker.py`:181-199), and the loop already accepts propose arguments as object or JSON string (lfm.py:502-504, 635-639).
- Tier 2 records cannot feed a multi-round builder today: TierRecord (nvsh/tiers/records.py:53-65) stores only the final operation/args, the per-round inspections Route collects (`_inspected`, router.py:478/500) are dropped before write, `operator_decision` is only approved/declined with no 'corrected' state and no id joining a proposal to its follow-up decision record (router.py:302-321), and `request_text` is stored only with `store_request_text`=true (default false, records.py:155-159).
- No real trajectories exist on this box to train from: ~/.local/state/nvsh/tiers.jsonl holds 147 Tier 2 (lfm) records, none with `operator_decision` set and none with `request_text` (no \[tiers\] block in the config, so `store_request_text` is false) -- counted read-only, contents not printed.
- A tuned model reaches Tier 2 only as merged full weights: no engine template passes --enable-lora (`runtime_docker.py` ENGINES); vLLM/SGLang get only an owner/name repo id with no revision pin and no host mount (`runtime_docker.py`:294-316, 181-200), so serving via vLLM needs the weights in a (private) HF repo, while llama-server mounts a local GGUF (`MODEL_MOUNT`, lines 70-71, 459-467) whose LFM2.5 conversion is unverified (docs/lfm-finetune.md:131).
- Local training on this DGX Spark (GB10, aarch64) is plausible but unverified for this stack: Needle3 LoRA trained here on the GPU (docs/needle-finetune.md:100-104, via jax\[cuda13\]), but no torch/unsloth/trl/peft is installed now, and the HF cache holds LiquidAI/LFM2.5-350M-Base, not the post-trained LFM2.5-350M the recipe targets (docs/lfm-finetune.md:62-67); the remote HF route (Route 3) is the documented fallback.
- The Tier 2 launcher passes no Hugging Face token into the vLLM container: `render_launch` (`runtime_docker.py`:509-531) adds only --user, `HF_HOME` and the `hf_cache_dir` mount (`_mount_args`, 484-493), and deliberately reads no environment -- so vLLM cannot pull a private jetson-ai-lab repo by itself; decision c20 only works if the tuned weights are already in `hf_cache_dir` (pre-fetched on the host with the operator's own hf login) and vLLM resolves them from the cache without a token.

- Multi-round inspect-then-decide trajectories (issue 39 work item 2) are deferred to a follow-up issue together with the TierRecord changes they need; this work tunes on single-turn propose/escalate plus explain examples.

The 2026-09-19 figures stay in the benchmark doc as history, not baseline.
- A successful fine-tune meets at least one of two bars, always with 0 wrong mutating proposals: (1) method validation -- the same recipe (split, template check, train, merge, export check, vLLM + lfm2 measurement) measurably improves LFM2.5-350M over stock on a public task with enough data; or (2) the nvsh use case improves dramatically, to at least >= 70% right proposals and >= 60% escalations on the committed test fold with a warm median < 500 ms. (1) without (2) is reported as 'the method works, nvsh's data is short', and the next step is collecting data (multi-round follow-up v2, issue 38), not more tuning.

- The method-validation task is Jetson Orin tool-calling first, built on NVIDIA's JetPack 7.2 agent skills (github.com/NVIDIA-AI-IOT/jetson-device-skills and jetson-bsp-skills); Jetson Orin knowledge Q&A is used only as extra explain examples, never as a success bar.

- Licensing and provenance ship as three artifacts: (1) the tuned model repo, licensed under LFM Open License 1.0 (never relabelled Apache-2.0 because the teachers are Apache), carrying the LFM licence, a modified-files notice, Liquid's retained notices and any NOTICE content, and stating the >= 10M USD commercial-use threshold that applies to every user; (2) a dataset and provenance record, with a manifest mapping each record to its source (for NVIDIA-derived text: repository, file, licence CC-BY-4.0 or Apache-2.0, transformed yes/no) and naming the generator, corrector and reviewer models; (3) a model card that lists Qwen 3.6/3.8, Gemma 4 and Nemotron 3.5 Lightning as synthetic-data generators and reviewers, not base models. Using CC-BY material only to evaluate does not make the model CC-BY.
